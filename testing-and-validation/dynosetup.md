# Testing and Validation Hardware

Now that the motors are built, test hardware will also be built to check how well and accurate to the analytical models the motors turned out to be.

I ended up building two rigs, one initially with an old car alternator, but after building and running tests I figured that I have two COTS brushless motors (the EaglePower LA8308), and it would be simpler to create a one-unit system with a smart coupling design to prevent too much misalignment and error from within the tester, and also easier to control and measure.

The car alternator dyno project has temporarily been put to the side, but would be useful for characterizing motors with a resistive force applied on it, being the energizable rotor windings of the alternator.

## Dynamometer 2: YVDyno-CS144 (Car Alternator to Absorption Dynamometer)

The idea here was to use my old car alternator as a generator, having previously torn down the parts and replaced the rectifier and brushes, and route the a +/- DC output through a power resistor to measure the DC voltage and current generated by spinning the test motor coupled to it.

However, I didn't create too intelligent of a method to line up the shafts, and the coupling I used would still have some misalignments and errors that were difficult to separate and keep consistent. Hence, I found it simpler of a test setup to create the YVDyno-8308, which I have used for the results.

You can still view the build of the YVDyno-CS144 [here](CS144-Alternator-to-Dyno.md).
